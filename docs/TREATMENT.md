# BUILT FOR TWO
A short film, about four minutes, drawn, scored and sung live in one HTML page. Raw WebGL2 and raw DSP. No libraries,
no assets, no recorded or generated media.

## Logline
Two old pelicans ride a red tandem across the mirror of a tidal flat, singing a song with a question and an answer.
Then one seat is empty. He carries the last thing she left, an egg in the basket, along the same road, singing the
questions and leaving her answers as silence. A storm nearly takes the egg. He catches it in his bill, and with his
bill closed he can only hum, so in the dark he hums both parts. At dawn the egg opens and the answer comes back.

## Four things are built for two. Each one loses its half, and each gets it back with a new meaning.
| thing | THEN | NOW | STORM / NIGHT | DAWN / RIDE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the tandem: rear pedals are chained to the front, they turn by themselves | four feet | rear pedals turn with nobody on them (absence) | pedals stop | pedals turn under a chick whose feet dangle above them (promise) |
| the long blue scarf, knitted for two necks | both ends worn | her end streams over an empty saddle | wrapped round the egg | round the chick's neck |
| the song: his question, her two notes "we two" (so-mi, the call every child knows) | duet | question, then a hole where the answer was | hummed through a closed bill, and he finally hums her notes himself | "wee-two!" |
| the bell on her handlebar | she rings it | silent. The VIEWER may ring it | torn at by wind | the chick rings it: the last sound of the film |

The reflection in the water keeps the same score: THEN two riders; NOW one; last shot, for a few seconds, the water shows
her on the back seat with her wing round the chick.

## Place
One place: a tidal flat. At low tide it is a mirror and there is a road. At high tide there is only sea. Home island behind
(a few lit houses), a lighthouse ahead. Tide = time.

## Acts
1. THEN (0:00-0:34.8) gold evening, mirror water, wide frame. Joy.
2. Match cut. NOW (0:34.8-1:25) same frame, same speed, dawn grey, one rider. The harmony's echo rings over the cut.
   Title. Verse two with holes. He cannot sing the word "you".
3. STORM (1:25-1:47.3) the tide comes over the causeway. "Hold on, little heart."
   Wave. CATCH (1:47.3-1:56) slow time, no music, the egg in the air, the pouch closes.
4. NIGHT (1:56-2:32) under the lighthouse. Each sweep of the beam lights the pouch from behind: the egg, and something
   moving in it. He hums. He waits for the answer. It does not come. He hums it himself.
5. DAWN (2:32-3:04) the shell cracks. "Who rides the morning?" A very long silence. "Wee-two."
6. RIDE (3:04-4:00) the whole band, the sentence he could not finish, the frame wide open.

## Cinema grammar
- Frame: 2.39 THEN, closes to 1.85 NOW, 1.33 STORM, nearly square NIGHT, opens to full width on "wee-two".
- Lens: THEN wide and together; NOW long and lonely; STORM hand-held; NIGHT locked off; DAWN one slow push; RIDE crane.
- Colour: gold -> slate -> bottle green -> indigo and amber -> rose and gold.
- The answer is heard on his face before we see who sang it.
- Last card: "In 1961 a computer learned to sing. The song was about a bicycle built for two."
